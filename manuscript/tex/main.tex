\title{Mean-Force Hamiltonians from Influence Functionals}
\author{Gerard McCaul}
% \affiliation{Affiliation}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The Hamiltonian of mean force (HMF) provides the standard starting point for strong-coupling thermodynamics, yet its explicit operator form is known for only a few solvable models. We present an exact construction of the HMF for a system coupled to a general environment using the imaginary-time influence functional. By applying a Hubbard--Stratonovich transformation, we represent the reduced equilibrium state as a stochastic average of quenched imaginary-time propagators. This representation isolates two logically independent obstructions to a compact HMF: noncommutativity of the system coupling operator with its bare Hamiltonian, and non-Gaussianity of the bath statistics. In the commuting (pure dephasing) sector, we derive a closed-form expression for the HMF where the temperature dependence is determined directly by the bath correlation kernel. For non-Gaussian baths, we show that the HMF can be systematically constructed via a nested Hubbard--Stratonovich hierarchy. We demonstrate this for a spin bath, recovering the leading non-Gaussian correction analytically in the commuting limit. Our results clarify that the locality of the HMF is governed by the algebraic closure of the system--bath interaction and the statistics of the environment rather than the coupling strength or temperature.
\end{abstract}
