\section{Discussion and outlook}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have presented the Quenched Density framework as a rigorous bridge between path-integral influence functionals and operator-level thermodynamics. By mapping the bath trace to a stochastic average over quenched propagators, we isolate the algebraic structure of the system's response from the statistical properties of the environment.

In the Gaussian commuting sector, this separation is perfectly clean: the bath statistics collapse to a scalar renormalization energy $\lambda$, and the system algebra reduces to a static potential shift $-\lambda f^2$. Our numerical benchmarks (Sec.~\ref{sec:numerical}) confirm that this framework reproduces the exact finite-bath physics to machine precision, establishing a "Standard Model" for strong-coupling validation.

The true power of this framework lies in its modularity, which naturally accommodates more general environments. Non-Gaussian statistics, for instance, can be handled by extending the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation to a nested hierarchy of auxiliary fields~\cite{mccaulHowWinFriends2021b}. In the Quenched Density picture, this generalizes the stochastic field $\xi(\tau)$ to a composite process without altering the fundamental definition of the reduced state as an average over propagators. Similarly, for non-commuting couplings ($[H_S, f] \neq 0$), the challenge shifts from the statistical generation of noise to the Lie-algebraic properties of the time-ordered effective propagator. The framework thus clearly delineates the statistical complexity of the bath from the dynamical complexity of the system response.

By solving the Gaussian commuting benchmark exactly, we have established the boundary condition that any generalized theory must satisfy. The Quenched Density framework proved here is ready to support these more complex physical regimes.
